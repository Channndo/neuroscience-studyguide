Core equations (Part 1)

Nernst Eion = (61.54 mV / z) · log10([ion]out / [ion]in)
Goldman (K⁺ + Na⁺, ignore Cl⁻) Vm = (RT/F) · ln( (PK[K⁺]o + PNa[Na⁺]o) / (PK[K⁺]i + PNa[Na⁺]i) )
Ionic current I = g · (Vm − Eion)  ·  τ = RmCm  ·  λ = √(Rm/Ri)
Passive steady state V∞ = Iinject · Rin   (if Rm doubles, V∞ doubles for fixed I)

Sign checks

  • ↑ Kout → EK less negative → Vm depolarizes.
  • ↑ Naout → ENa more positive.
  • Vm = Eion → zero driving force for that ion.
  • Na⁺/K⁺ pump: 3 Na out, 2 K in (electrogenic).

Quick Part 2 · minimal

  • Evoked PSP ≈ n · q (quanta × mini size).
  • EPSPs/IPSPs sum within τ (temporal) and at soma (spatial).
  • NMDA LTP: glutamate + depolarization → Mg²⁺ unblock → Ca²⁺ in.
  • Shunt inhibition: ↑ g → ↓ Rin → smaller EPSPs.

Units (common trap)

  • 1 nS × 1 mV = 1 pA  |  50 nS × (−70 mV) = −3.5 nA
  • IC = Cm · dVm/dt at step onset

Worked examples (show your steps on the exam)

Example A — Nernst + Goldman resting Vm

Given at 37 °C: [K⁺]i = 140, [K⁺]o = 5, [Na⁺]i = 15, [Na⁺]o = 145 mM; PK : PNa = 1 : 0.05; ignore Cl⁻.

  1. EK = 61.54 · log10(5/140) ≈ −89.1 mV
  2. ENa = 61.54 · log10(145/15) ≈ +60.6 mV
  3. Goldman: num = 1·5 + 0.05·145 = 12.25; den = 1·140 + 0.05·15 = 140.75
  4. Vm = 61.54 · log10(12.25/140.75) ≈ −65.3 mV ( nearer EK than ENa )

Do not plug “average of EK and ENa” unless the question gives only permeability ratios of the E values — use concentrations in Goldman.

Example B — Current from conductance

gNa = 50 nS, Vm = −10 mV, ENa = +60 mV (outward positive convention).

  1. Driving force: Vm − ENa = −10 − 60 = −70 mV
  2. I = 50 × 10−9 S × (−70 × 10−3) V = −3.5 × 10−9 A = −3.5 nA (inward Na⁺)

Example C — Concentration fold-change (no calculator friendly)

Extracellular Na⁺ doubled, intracellular unchanged → ENa shifts by 61.54·log10(2) ≈ +18.5 mV (more positive). Fourfold extracellular K⁺ → EK shifts ≈ +37 mV (less negative).
