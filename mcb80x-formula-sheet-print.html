Formula & Constants Reference

Quick reference for proctored-exam calculations · 37 °C unless noted

Symbols follow course usage: Vm = membrane potential; Eion = reversal (Nernst) potential for that ion; g = conductance (S), R = resistance (Ω), Cm = membrane capacitance. Outward current is often defined positive.

Part 1 — Electrical properties

Nernst (equilibrium) potential — monovalent cation Eion = (RT / zF) · ln([ion]out / [ion]in)
Log10 form: Eion = (61.5 mV / z) · log10([ion]out / [ion]in) at 37 °C

Anions (e.g. Cl−, z = −1): use the same magnitude with inside/outside in the log; sign follows your convention for ECl. Divalent Ca2+: divide the 61.5 mV slope by 2. Doubling [out] shifts E by ≈ +18 mV (z = 1) or ≈ +9 mV per 2× for z = 2.

Goldman (constant-field) resting potential Vm = (RT/F) · ln( (PK[K+]out + PNa[Na+]out + …) / (PK[K+]in + PNa[Na+]in + …) )

With only K+ and Na+ and PCl ≈ 0, compute EK, ENa first, then apply permeability weights. Resting Vm lies between EK and ENa, nearer the ion with higher P.

Ohm’s law for an open ion channel Iion = gion · (Vm − Eion)

Driving force = (Vm − Eion). At Vm = Eion, net ionic current through that conductance is zero. Example: 50 nS × (−70 mV) = −3.5 nA.

Membrane time constant τ = Rm · Cm

Larger τ → slower voltage changes for a given current. If Rm doubles with fixed injected current I, steady-state V = I·Rm doubles and τ doubles.

Length (space) constant λ = √(Rm / Ri)   (units: distance along cable)

Rm = specific membrane resistance × geometry; Ri = axial resistance. Higher leak (lower Rm) → shorter λ. Larger diameter → lower Ri → longer λ.

Membrane capacitance (parallel-plate model) Cm ∝ A / d   (area / membrane thickness)

Thicker membrane → lower capacitance per unit area. Myelin increases effective Rm and reduces capacitive load at internodes.

Steady-state passive response to step current V∞ = Iinject · Rin   where Rin is input resistance

Part 1 — Sign checks (no calculator)

  • Raising extracellular K+ → EK less negative → Vm depolarizes toward new EK.
  • Raising extracellular Na+ → ENa more positive.
  • At rest, Vm negative: opening Na+ channels → depolarization toward ENa.
  • Opening K+ channels when Vm > EK → hyperpolarizing/outward K+ current.
  • Na+/K+ pump: 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in per cycle → electrogenic, hyperpolarizing contribution.

Part 2 — Synapses & integration (useful relations)

Quantal (mini) postsynaptic events Evoked PSP ≈ n · q   (n vesicles released × mean mini amplitude q)

Stochastic n explains trial-to-trial variability. Facilitation/depression change effective n or release probability p.

Summation (conceptual) Vm(t) integrates EPSPs/IPSPs within τ; spatial sum at soma/AIS from multiple synapses

Inhibitory shunt: increased conductance lowers effective Rin and attenuates EPSPs ( divisive + subtractive effects).

NMDA receptor (LTP gate) Requires glutamate + postsynaptic depolarization to relieve Mg2+ block → Ca2+ entry

Part 3 — Sensory & misc.

  • Weber–Fechner (concept): perceived intensity ∝ log(stimulus strength) — explains compressive coding.
  • Cochlea: place code — basal = high f, apical = low f; not a single exam identity formula.
  • Retina: photoreceptor dark current via cGMP-gated channels; light → ↓ cGMP → hyperpolarization.
  • Ohm for dB (auditory): intensity level IL = 10·log10(I/I0) dB — know log-compression idea.

Unit reminders

  • 1 S = 1 A/V; 1 nS × 1 mV = 1 pA; 1 nS × 70 mV = 70 pA = 0.07 nA.
  • Capacitive current IC = Cm · dVm/dt (transient at step onset).
